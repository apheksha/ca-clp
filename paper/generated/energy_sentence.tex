We re-ran the oracle on the 90 missions with an energy budget for every combination of $k_v$ scaled by $\{0, 0.5, 1, 2, 4\}$ and a per-waypoint overhead $k_w$ of 0, 1, 2, 5\% of the mission's witness route length. With the budget held fixed, 14--100\% of these missions stayed feasible; with the budget rescaled to keep each mission's slack over its witness, all of them did. Re-verifying the 301 released airport plans that carry an energy budget: without a waypoint overhead their verdict changed for at most 0.3\% of plans (climb weight up to 4$\times$), but an overhead of 1--5\% of the route length per waypoint would have rejected 3--64\% of them, because detour-based plans have many waypoints. The energy verdict therefore depends on modeling choices in a way the zone verdict does not.
